# Filozy document-use policy review

Reviewed September 9–10, 2026; policy revision September 10, 2026. This is a release and legal-review checklist, not a legal opinion, worldwide compliance certification or guarantee against claims.

## Published scope

- Keep the approved public operator label **Filozy** and contact **admin.filozy@gmail.com**. Do not infer a registered company, address, governing jurisdiction or professional certification.
- Privacy explains local document processing, temporary device storage, separate email attachments, hosting information, security limitations, lawful requests and individual rights.
- Terms explain document permissions, unacceptable use, backups, output verification, electronic-signature and redaction limits, reasonable service limitations and a qualified business-loss exclusion.
- No blanket waiver of liability for uploaded documents, consumer indemnity, zero-dollar liability cap, forced arbitration, foreign exclusive venue or claim deadline has been added. Document-user responsibilities do not excuse Filozy's own legally actionable conduct.
- Public URL conversion remains unavailable. Its retention and provider disclosures must be reviewed before activation. Ads remain disabled; the existing AdSense readiness checks must continue passing.
- Preserve the existing design, logo, fonts, paths, file-processing logic and device-data controls. Add a Terms link beside Privacy in the shared navigation so visitors can find both from individual tools. These changes are explanatory, not new collection or consent mechanisms.

## Still requires the operator and qualified counsel

1. **Legal identity and location:** confirm the actual contracting party, establishment, trading-name status and legally required address/contact disclosures. The brand label alone is not evidence that identity obligations are met. Do not replace the approved public label or publish personal details without the owner's direction.
2. **Applicable markets:** review the free/ad-supported service model against the countries actually served or targeted, consumer and small-business protections, children's use, electronic signatures, document misuse, accessibility duties and any local registration or representation obligations. Availability on the internet does not establish one governing law for every user.
3. **Contract notice and agreement:** assess whether existing footer links and terms presentation create an enforceable agreement in relevant markets. If affirmative acceptance is needed, design a clear pre-use notice and proportionate evidence process before adding it. A silent page visit is not a verified consent record; do not add a checkbox or tracking just to claim enforcement.
4. **Actual privacy operations:** confirm purposes and lawful bases for correspondence and connection data, retention rules, access controls, rights-request handling, breach response, provider roles/contracts and international-transfer safeguards. Update the public notice to match verified arrangements; a provider's privacy link is not a substitute for Filozy's duties. Do not invent a DPO, representative, transfer mechanism or compliance certification.
5. **Liability and insurance:** have counsel assess the qualified business-loss clause and appropriate consumer/business terms. Evaluate technology errors-and-omissions and cyber cover if appropriate. Neither an insurance policy nor a disclaimer prevents a claim or replaces secure operation.
6. **Content and IP:** retain software/font notices and asset provenance, review marketing claims and handle credible rights complaints. This document is not a plagiarism clearance or a statutory copyright safe-harbour registration.
7. **Change gates:** review again before enabling ads, accounts, payments, server document uploads, cloud storage, AI processing, a public sharing system, or a new hosting/email provider. Do not activate ads merely because a domain is connected; follow `ADSENSE_SETUP.md`.

## Official sources informing this baseline

These sources illustrate important limits, not an assessment of every country's law. Guidance and local applicability must be rechecked for a launch or material service change.

- [UK CMA: writing a fair contract for customers](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/writing-a-fair-contract-for-customers): broad exclusions can be unfair; explain limits plainly and preserve legally required responsibilities. A generic “as far as law permits” sentence alone does not cure an overbroad disclaimer.
- [European Commission: Unfair Contract Terms Directive](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/consumer-protection-law/consumer-contract-law/unfair-contract-terms-directive_en): unfair standard consumer terms are not binding; use intelligible terms and preserve access to remedies.
- [Australian ACCC: contracts](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/buying-products-and-services/contracts): standard-form contract terms may be unfair; consumer rights cannot simply be removed by website terms.
- [Canada OPC: accountability](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/p_principle/principles/p_accountability/): where PIPEDA applies, outsourcing processing does not remove accountability for information under the organisation's control.
- [UK ICO: required privacy information](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/individual-rights/the-right-to-be-informed/what-privacy-information-should-we-provide/): identity, purposes, applicable bases, recipients, retention, rights and relevant international transfers must reflect real processing. The ICO marks this guidance as under review following the Data (Use and Access) Act.
