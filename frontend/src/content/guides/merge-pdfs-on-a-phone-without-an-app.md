You have three PDFs on your phone — a scanned form, a photographed receipt, a letter someone emailed — and the portal wants one file. The app store offers you a merge app with a subscription and an account. You do not need it. Your phone's browser can do the whole job on the device, and the files never go anywhere.

## Before you start

Have the PDFs somewhere the browser's file picker can reach:

- **iPhone and iPad**: the Files app (iCloud Drive, On My iPhone, or any connected storage). Email attachments can be saved to Files with the share button.
- **Android**: Downloads, Drive, or any folder the Files app shows. Attachments save to Downloads.

Photos of documents are not PDFs yet. Turn them into one with [JPG to PDF](/jpg-to-pdf/) first, or photograph pages directly with [Scan to PDF](/scan-pdf/), which straightens and sharpens them on the device.

## Step by step

1. Open [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf/) in Safari or Chrome.
2. Tap the file area. The system picker appears; select every PDF you want to combine. On iOS, tap *Select* in the picker to choose several; on Android, long-press the first and tap the others.
3. The pages appear as thumbnails. **Drag** a page — or a whole file's group — to change the order. Pinch or use the size control if thumbnails are too small to tell apart.
4. Tap the run button. The merge happens in the tab; a receipt shows how many document bytes were sent, which is zero.
5. **Save.** Safari offers a download that lands in Files; Chrome on Android saves to Downloads. From there you can share it to the portal, email or a message.

If the order needs work after merging — a page rotated the wrong way, two pages swapped — [Organize PDF pages](/organise-pdf/) and [Rotate PDF](/rotate-pdf/) fix it on the phone too, and the connected-workflow buttons pass the merged file to them without re-selecting it.

## What to expect

- **Speed.** A few text documents merge in a second. Scans are bigger and take longer, but merging does not re-render pages, so even large files are quick unless memory runs short.
- **Memory.** The phone holds every page in the tab's memory. A hundred full-page colour scans at 600 DPI may exceed what the browser allows. Run the largest file through [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf/) first, or merge in batches.
- **Background tabs.** Mobile browsers pause background tabs. Keep the page in front until the download prompt appears.
- **Wi-Fi.** The site loads once; the merge itself needs no connection. Airplane mode after the page has loaded will not stop it.

## Why this is safe to do with a real document

An app or an online service that merges your files has your files. The browser approach never sends them anywhere: the PDFs are read into the tab's memory, combined there, and written back to the phone. Open the browser's developer tools on a laptop some time and watch the Network panel while merging — [here is how](/guides/how-to-check-if-a-pdf-tool-uploads-your-file/). On the phone, the receipt under the result is the same information in one line.
